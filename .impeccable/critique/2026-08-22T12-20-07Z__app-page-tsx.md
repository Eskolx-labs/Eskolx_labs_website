## Design Specificity Verdict

Authored for this product: the almanac-as-two-fields concept is owned end to end (body fields, engraved vine plate, ten-berry Eshcol cluster, tabular figures, grower's-entry mission copy). Not a category-interchangeable dark-SaaS skin. The one near-miss: the tier/roadmap stacks and hero pin run generic scrubbed timelines whose pacing and amplitude are not tuned to the almanac metaphor — the identity is specific, the motion is scrollytelling-by-numbers.

Deterministic scan (B): 0 findings, but the detector is degraded (missing parser modules → regex fallback; undercount, not a clean bill). No page errors, one expected 404 (Vercel insights), no overflow at 1440/390, all three pins scrub (verified transform matrices), field chain correct, contrast 6.51:1 (ink-soft on parchment) and 5.30:1 (vermilion button) — both AA.

## Overall Impression

The page has the taste of the references but not their physics. Minimal-goods and basement commit: big scale, fast boundary turns, layered velocities, compositions that keep resolving for the full length of their pins. Eskolx spends its scroll budget on compositions that finish early and delivers its own central metaphor — the turn of the almanac — as the slowest, least committed motion on the page. The assets would survive a tier upgrade; the motion is what must be rebuilt.

## What's Working

1. The two-field system executes with total consistency — every surface tints from `--field-*` on body; flips chain correctly in both directions, verified live end to end.
2. The first viewport is a real moment — masked three-line reveal + vine rise + oversize logo settling 1.9→1 is the one section that competes with the references.
3. Asset craft is the differentiator — the hand-authored vine plate (trunk, forks, midribs, ten berries, tendrils, `currentColor` inking/gilding) is reference-grade.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The hero pin dies at 52% of its room.** Line masks complete by 38%, sub faded by 52%, cue gone by 18%; the remaining ~1.4 viewports show a static headline, a creeping vine, and a mud-colored page. Re-chart the beats across the whole room: lines 0–60/15–75/30–90 with a scale push before release, sub 60–100, vine with scale 0.6→1 and rotation. → /impeccable animate

**[P0] The field flip is a 2700px dissolve, not a page turn.** Measured mid-pin body color rgb(86,71,55) — neither parchment nor loam — held for a full viewport. The central metaphor reads as the opposite of committed. Compress flips into a ~12% boundary window with ease-in so the color commits like a wheel flip, then holds. → /impeccable animate

**[P1] Amplitudes are timid and there is zero differential parallax.** Every tween is single-layer `ease: linear`; the largest scale event is the nav logo. Minimal-goods grows a circle to fullscreen and runs columns at different speeds; basement runs layered scenes. Give each visible element its own speed inside a pin (headline ×1.2, vine ×0.7, plate ×1.0) and raise the floor (cards from y 60–80 + scale 0.92; vine from scale 0.6). → /impeccable bolder / overdrive

**[P1] Stake/phase lighting is a snap, not a state.** Waypoint duration 0.3 timeline units ≈ 11px of scroll; the wine border/background flashes in one wheel click. Make them 6–10-unit scrubbed windows with staggered border → bg → text so lighting drifts with the scroll like the stack does. → /impeccable animate

**[P2] Desktop tier rail has a ~1700px dead zone and mobile pins violate the DESIGN.md Mobile Pin Rule.** The stake rail stretches to the 2162px plate window; the stack travel (-75%) leaves 1620px of empty plate. At 390×844 the tier window shows a 261px visible band while the rail sits 220px above; Casey scrolls a 3780px room to read slivers. Distribute stakes vertically, drive the stack to -100% or shorten the window; on <lg convert tiers/roadmap to ordinary stacked sections. → /impeccable layout / adapt

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan (first-timer):** Hooked at the first viewport, but the dead second half of the hero breaks the spell before he reaches the origin — he arrives at "The Eshcol Identity" already bored, fatal to the reveal he should be hungry for.
- **Riley (stress tester):** Flicks hard through the pins and sees everything — linear tweens, snap stakes, single-speed layers. Nothing overshoots, nothing resists; reads as GSAP defaults, not choreography.
- **Casey (mobile):** Tier and roadmap pins effectively broken (slivers + offscreen rail); the vine — the brand asset — is hidden below lg; 14,665px of scrolling on a 844px viewport.

## Minor Observations

- One console 404 (Vercel, harmless).
- Reduced-motion keeps the 300vh/420vh shells: RM users scroll 2700px of sticky static hero (timelines skipped, wrapper heights remain).
- Nav and hero both carry a vermilion "Explore GitHub" at y=0 — a literal violation of the One Vermilion Rule on the first spread.
- `html { scroll-behavior: smooth }` coexists with Lenis; anchors settled in ~1.3s via native smoothing — the two smoothing systems can fight.
- The footer seal's -4deg landing is the only moment with personality easing — a genuinely charming beat.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the almanac's central promise is "the page turns," why is the turn a 2700px ooze instead of a crisp flip you feel?
2. The vine is the signature asset — why does it animate only in the hero and sit statue-still in leadership and the footer?
3. If Casey can't see the tier plate she's supposed to be reading, does the tier pin exist for her at all — or is it a 3780px scroll tax?
