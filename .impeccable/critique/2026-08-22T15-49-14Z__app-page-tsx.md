## Design Specificity Verdict

Round 3 is the round the machine became honest. All three pins measure exactly their promised rooms (2700/3780/3240 at 1440x900) and every scrubbed timeline lands its final value at pin release (tier stack reaches exactly -1620px at 100%, roadmap completes its two-page turn exactly at release). The field controller commits one 0.55s power2.inOut turn at the boundary (measured: body bg #ece1c6 → #241407 in a single step between scrollY 1204–1445, no dissolve window). Reduced-motion renders static complete states (seal opacity 1, transform none, verified). Mobile collapses pins to stacked sections (hero 688px, tiers 2844px, roadmap 1418px rooms) with zero horizontal overflow.

The design language remains genuinely specific: two fields, single engraved vine, one vermilion CTA per spread, hairline/tick/hatch grammar, tabular mono labels. No generic dark-SaaS anywhere. What still lags the references is not correctness — it is velocity discipline: every scrubbed tween in the system is ease: linear on a single transform axis, so the page is always legible, always calm, and always the same speed. Basement's dynamism is independent layers moving at different velocities with eased resolves; this page choreographs one motion at a time.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Mobile hero seal collides with the sub-copy.** Mobile seal rect (top 392, bottom 448, left 302–358) overlaps the sub paragraph's final line (sub bottom 421). The round-3 mobile seal (bottom-60 right-8, hero.tsx:72) sits on the closing words "…implemented, tested, and understood." Fix: bottom-60 right-8 → bottom-72 right-8, or cap the sub at max-w-[85%].
2. **[P1] Monophonic velocity.** All scrubbed Animation tweens default ease: 'linear' (lib/scrollytelling.tsx:120,188); the only eased motion on the page is the field flip (field-controller.ts:71). Basement's dynamism is layered: eased resolves, differential parallax per column. Concrete: pass eases into Animation (power2.out on the vine settle, sine.inOut on the tier stack), and give each pin a second velocity layer (the hero's headline yPercent 6 is the only differential parallax in the build).
3. **[P2] The tier pin's heart is a single-rate slide.** 4,200px of room with one constant -75% slide and 4 stake lights; 1.5 viewports between stake events with no resolving gesture. Add a mid-pin beat: parallax the stake rail (slower) against the plate, or a masked context reveal at 50%, or a grape-swell at 75% to carry the brand through the room.
4. **[P2] No journey progress in a 12,900px scroll.** The only "where am I" is intra-scene dots and the stake rail. A fixed hairline page-rule in the nav (almanac register) that fills as sections pass would answer heuristic 1 with zero clutter. Also the roadmap exit panel (bg-parchment, roadmap.tsx:121) sits outside the field-zone system: the body flips to loam a half-viewport before the leadership boundary while the exit panel is still parchment.
5. **[P3] Mobile theater absent by rule.** The Mobile Pin Rule is correct, but the mobile seal/vine have no presence; a one-time seal stamp on first scroll would give Casey the one moment she gets.

## VERSUS THE REFERENCE

The references work at three gears Eskolx is missing:
1. **Differential parallax** — minimal-goods runs columns at different speeds; basement runs rails inside rails. Eskolx has exactly one differential pair (headline yPercent 6, hero.tsx:89-92). The tier stack (concept-tiers.tsx:187-192) and roadmap (roadmap.tsx:106) are single translates. Add a second layer and the rooms stop feeling like tracks.
2. **Eased resolution** — basement's scrubbed timelines end with power2 settles; the hero seal (hero.tsx:58-75) resolves linearly 2.6 → 1. The read is a program, not a score. Ease is already plumbed (lib/scrollytelling.tsx:187-188) — use it.
3. **Mid-scene gesture** — basement never lets a 400vh room play one 0-100 curve. The tier pin is 4,200px of one linear motion with 4 stake lights (concept-tiers.tsx:203-214). The stakes should be a side-channel moving at their own speed, not a checkpoint queue.
- Where Eskolx wins: pin honesty (rooms match promises), single-writer field control, reduced-motion statics, a proprietary visual language — all things the references only imply. The gap is density of motion, not correctness.
